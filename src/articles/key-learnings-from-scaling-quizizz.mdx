# Key Learnings from Scaling Quizizz

Someone remarkable recently asked me what I learned from scaling Quizizz. I did not have a great answer in the moment, so I spent some time thinking it through. Here is the distillation.

## Execution is everything

Ideas are cheap until they solve a real problem. Strong belief helps, but it is not proof. If you cannot make something work in the real world, it stays an opinion.

## Failure is a luxury

Intuition gives you ideas, but most ideas fail. You will fail far more often than you succeed. The rare wins matter, but the losses are what sharpen your judgment.

Failure is also a privilege. Not everyone gets to fail and keep playing.

## Survive

Time is limited. Bets are limited. Move fast, fail fast, fix fast, and when needed, kill fast.

Stay focused on impact. Avoid busy work and attachment to your own idea. In wartime, that discipline matters most. In peacetime, the same instincts need a different balance.

## AI is a strategic advantage

AI increases the number of bets you can place. Use it fully.

It should accelerate experimentation and increase the rate of learning. If you become a bottleneck, your team will route around you.

## Team sports

Startups are a team sport. Hiring the right people at the right time for the right roles is hard, and the bar has to stay high as you scale.

Growth often follows a win, and complacency is the danger after that. Lead with respect, but do not tolerate mediocrity. Everyone should own the outcome and trust others to do the same.

## 10x teams, not 10x people

AI is changing org design and the old man-month thinking even more than before.

A small, highly motivated team using AI well can outperform a much larger team that is not. At minimum, teams that use AI will keep pulling ahead of teams that do not.

## Executive athlete

Chaos is part of the system. Accept it.

Not everything will be under control, and that is okay. This is a long game, so protect your energy and clarity. Your team needs you to stay sane.

## Customer obsession is survival

If you only focus on acquiring new customers, existing ones will leave. Growth will stall, and a leaky bucket never fills.

Deep customer focus buys time, trust, and more chances to win.
